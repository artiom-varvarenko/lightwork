# Battery-powered breadboard LED

Original procedure and trial scripts by the Lightwork contributors, 2026. Licence: CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. No third-party circuit instructions or photographs are reproduced.

## Scope, materials and safety

Use a standard solderless breadboard whose labelled rows 1–30 each connect a–e together and f–j separately; a 5 mm red LED; a 1 kΩ resistor rated at least 0.25 W; one insulated return jumper; and a switched two-AA holder (nominal 3 V) with insulated leads. Use intact cells of the same type. No mains, wall adapters, lithium cells, soldering, external instruments powering the circuit, or exposed high voltage. Never put a bare wire straight across the battery. Disconnect/remove batteries before moving any part. If anything warms, smells or leaks, switch off, disconnect and stop.

An operator must check the breadboard's actual continuity pattern and row labels before use. Do not assume split power rails are continuous; this design avoids the rails entirely. Resistor current limiting is mandatory: at 3 V, 1 kΩ limits current to at most 3 mA even with the LED replaced by a short. A resistor value cannot be established reliably by a distant camera; verify it from its label or with a meter while unpowered. A dim LED may be hard to see under projection.

Fix the board with row 1 at camera-top and columns a–e camera-left. Define `board-zone`, `row-10`, `row-15`, `row-20`, `resistor-zone`, `led-zone`, `return-zone`, `battery-zone`. Accept close-up manual layout; individual holes may be too small for reliable projected guidance. Use a printed row/column label beside the board and operator inspection. Nouns include breadboard, resistor, red LED, jumper wire, battery holder.

## Seven steps (preserve these IDs)

1. `setup`: Battery holder OFF and batteries removed. Secure the empty board. Confirm the parts, row labels, disconnected holder and checked 1 kΩ resistor. Use present checks and a Meta no-contradiction reading. If polarity or continuity markings cannot be inspected, stop.
2. `resistor`: With power disconnected, insert the resistor between a10 and a15. Its polarity does not matter. The leads must be in different numbered rows, not the same connected strip. Use presence in `resistor-zone` and a Meta placement check. Correct errors only with power disconnected.
3. `led`: Insert LED anode in e15 and cathode in e20. For a new untrimmed LED the longer lead is normally anode; the flat side normally marks cathode. Operator checks the actual part's markings/datasheet; do not rely on lead length alone for a reused part. Use present in `led-zone` plus a Meta orientation/row check. If visible evidence is insufficient, report unsure; never infer polarity from illumination alone.
4. `return-jumper`: Put the insulated return jumper between a20 and a25. Battery holder remains disconnected. Use presence in `return-zone` and a Meta endpoint check. Reject a jumper connecting row 10 directly to row 25, which would bypass the load once the battery is connected.
5. `polarity-review`: While still disconnected, operator traces the entire path: positive lead will enter c10; resistor connects row 10 to row 15; LED anode on row 15 and cathode on row 20; jumper joins row 20 to row 25; negative lead will enter c25. Confirm resistor value, LED polarity, no touching bare leads and no direct battery short. This is a confirm-only safety checkpoint and is logged unverified; a camera/model cannot certify electrical safety. Do not connect power until this inspection is complete.
6. `power-on`: Holder OFF; insert red positive lead at c10 and black negative lead at c25, then insert the two AA cells according to holder markings. Operator rechecks before switching ON briefly. The expected visible result is one red LED glowing steadily. Use a Meta reading and declared presence/activity evidence; if the LED is dark, flickering, hidden or too dim, stop rather than increasing voltage. Switch OFF, remove batteries and troubleshoot unpowered. A lit LED alone does not prove resistor value or safe wiring.
7. `power-off`: Switch OFF and remove the batteries before touching the wiring. Operator confirms disconnection; this final confirm-only step is unverified, not a model-certified power isolation check.

Planted mistakes are staged ONLY with batteries removed: absent resistor, LED in the wrong numbered row, reversed LED, or jumper in the wrong row. Capture those trials unpowered. Restore and manually inspect the full circuit before any powered trial. Never deliberately energise a short circuit or a circuit without its resistor. Hard-case labels are unsure when the required pin/row is hidden, not confident guesses. This is a short assembly snippet, not a general electronics safety tool.
